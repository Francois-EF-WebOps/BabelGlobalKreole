# Language relationships

Relationships are typed, sourced and scoped. Do not force every language into one family tree.

## Relationship types
`genealogical_claim`, `contact_influence`, `lexical_borrowing`, `structural_comparison`, `shared_feature`, `mutual_intelligibility_claim`, `design_inspiration`, `standardization_target`, `fictional_adaptation`, `application_uses`, `disputed_relationship`.

Each relationship records direction where meaningful, sources, scope, evidence status, rationale and counterevidence where available.

## Rules
1. Similar words do not prove common ancestry.
2. Contact hypotheses need more than geographic proximity.
3. Feature comparisons identify feature, variety, period and source.
4. “Influenced by” does not mean “derived entirely from”.
5. A shared proposed form is a design decision, not historical evidence.
6. Preserve variation, register, context and speaker/community attribution where known.
7. Record disagreement rather than forcing a binary answer.

A fictional language can have a `design_inspiration` relationship to real languages without being described as their historical descendant.
