# TikTok test 01 — What makes a language tool trustworthy?

**Campaign:** BabelGlobalKreole fast-feedback pilot  
**Post ID:** BGK-TT-001  
**Status:** awaiting human approval — do not publish automatically  
**Goal:** collect useful feedback from speakers, learners and researchers; this is a discovery test, not a claim of representativeness.

## Video script (about 18–22 seconds)

**On-screen opening:**  
When a digital tool handles your language, what makes you trust it?

**Voiceover / on-screen text:**  
“Quick question for people who speak or study Creole languages. Should a language tool force one ‘correct’ form—or show regional variants and where each form comes from?

I’m building BabelGlobalKreole as an evidence-led project. I don’t want to invent words or treat all Creoles as one language.

What matters most: sources, local variants, speaker review—or all three?”

**Final on-screen prompt:**  
Comment: SOURCES / VARIANTS / SPEAKER REVIEW / ALL THREE

## Caption

A language tool should be useful without pretending that uncertainty or variation does not exist. What would make you trust one? If you’re comfortable, mention the language or community you’re thinking of. We’re gathering perspectives, not claiming this small comment sample represents every speaker.

Project: https://github.com/Francois-EF-WebOps/BabelGlobalKreole
